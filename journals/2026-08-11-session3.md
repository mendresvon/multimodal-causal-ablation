# 2026-08-11 (session 3)

### 1. Objective & Hypothesis

Session 2 rewrote the measurement layer and left one experiment outstanding: the random-ablation null that decides whether Week 3's transfer ratio means anything. This session ran it, read the answer, and then did the unglamorous half of the work, bringing the repository into a state where the numbers can actually be cited in a paper.

The hypothesis under test was the one session 2 could not settle. `R` is the ratio of the fine-tuned model's probability drop to the base model's drop under the same five-dimension ablation. Every class returned `R > 1`, and the substrate bands read that as preservation. But the fine-tuned model is more fragile to `a_cls` ablation in general, so `R > 1` could be nothing more than that global fragility. The null was designed to separate the two: if the base model's probe-selected top-5 carries class-specific transferred structure, its `R` must sit above the `R` produced by five arbitrary dimensions.

It does not. The finding is negative and it is clean.

A second, unplanned objective emerged partway through: `results/measurement_resolution.json` cited "WITHDRAWN (ADR 0006)" while no ADR 0006 existed anywhere. That dangling citation had already been committed.

The notebook was run in the runtime with the null and rank-validation cells that session 2 had instrumented but not executed.

- Null: 2000 random 5-dimension subsets of the 64-dim `a_cls`, seed `20260812`, held deliberately separate from the measurement bootstrap's seed `20260811`. ### 4. Empirical Findings & Data Links

**The transfer ratio does not clear its null in any class.**

| class | base drop | FT drop | R | R 95% CI | R_null | R_null 95% CI |
| :--- | ---: | ---: | ---: | :--- | ---: | :--- |
| anger | 4.3286 | 7.3154 | 1.6900 | [1.1962, 2.4108] | 1.4150 | [1.3993, 1.4311] |
| disgust | 2.3000 | 4.7920 | 2.0835 | [1.4680, 2.9032] | 2.0050 | [1.9879, 2.0232] |
| fear | 0.6387 | 2.7714 | 4.3393 | [2.6772, 9.3368] | 4.0093 | [3.8999, 4.1363] |
| happiness | 2.0433 | 2.8454 | 1.3926 | [0.7825, 2.3622] | 1.1852 | [1.1591, 1.2108] |
| sadness | 4.0641 | 5.3542 | 1.3174 | [1.0768, 1.7162] | 1.2073 | [1.1951, 1.2195] |
| surprise | 2.2882 | 4.7421 | 2.0724 | [1.5534, 2.5328] | 1.7256 | [1.7080, 1.7435] |

**0 of 6 classes have an `R` whose 95% CI clears the null, and 0 of 6 fall below it.** The base model's top-5 audio dimensions, ablated inside the fine-tuned model, do no more damage than five arbitrary audio dimensions.

Two details deserve to survive into the write-up. Fear's `R` of 4.34 is the largest in the table and would have been the headline under the old bands, its null is 4.01. The ratio is large because the denominator is 0.6387 pp; a small denominator inflates random subsets exactly as it inflates selected ones, which is precisely what a null is for. Happiness fails in the other direction: its CI lower bound of 0.7825 is below 1, so the interval is consistent with the fine-tuned model taking *less* damage, and that class carries no directional information even before the null comparison.

All six point estimates nonetheless sit above their null. No CI separates them, because the `R` intervals are wide (24 test samples per class) while the null intervals are tight (2000 subsets). "No per-class significance" is the conclusion adopted, but the 6-of-6 direction belongs in the paper rather than being left for a reader to notice.

**Top-5 overlap is at or below chance for half the classes.** Anger 3, surprise 3, fear 2, disgust 1, sadness 1, happiness 0. Selectivity-vector cosine similarity runs 0.9602–0.9896 with matched minus mismatched at +1.168221021057142, high, but correlational, and not a substitute for the causal test.

**The probe ranking does not track measured ablation damage.** Day 13b, all 12 model×class pairs: `probe_over_measured` runs 0.1585 (fine-tuned disgust) to 0.7305 (base surprise), so every probe-selected top-5 does less damage than a top-5 chosen by measured effect, in some cases by more than 6×. Spearman rank correlation between the two orderings runs −0.5438 to +0.0023, negative or nil in all 12. `|L1 probe weight|` does not predict ablation damage magnitude, and the transfer analysis assumed it did.

### 6. Interpretability Retrospective

The instructive part of this session is how close the project came to publishing the opposite result. Every ingredient of a positive finding was present: six classes with `R > 1`, a taxonomy that labelled all six *Substrate Preservation*, and cosine similarities of 0.96–0.99 between the two models' selectivity vectors. Nothing in that picture is wrong as arithmetic. It is simply not a test, because the bands only cover `R <= 1` and every measured value lies above 1, a column with one value in all six rows discriminates nothing.

What made it a test was measuring what arbitrary dimensions do. Once the null is in place the result inverts, and the honest reading is that the base model's probe-selected audio dimensions carry no transferred class-specific structure detectable at this sample size.

The Day 13b check adds a second reason for caution that is independent of the null. If `|probe weight|` ranked causally important dimensions, a set chosen by measured effect should not out-damage the probe's own pick, and it does, in all 12 pairs, with rank correlations at or below zero. The two probes are fit on different activations, so this is not proof that either probe is wrong; but the transfer analysis assumed each model's top-5 *is* its causally important set, and that assumption is what fails. Both failures point the same way.

Weeks 0–2 are untouched by any of this and remain publishable: the audio CLS carries class-specific causal weight, four of six classes clear a 2.5× selectivity ratio in the base model, and the dose-response curve has real confidence intervals behind it.
