### 1. Objective & Hypothesis

Session 1 cleared the Day 0 fidelity gate and ran Weeks 2–4 to completion. The accuracy
numbers it produced looked right; the statistics derived from them did not. The objective of
this session was to find out why, fix it, and re-run.

The hypothesis was that the defect was arithmetic rather than experimental — that the
ablation harness was measuring the model correctly and the reporting layer on top of it was
manufacturing confidence. That hypothesis was confirmed, and the root cause turned out to be
a single fact about the split: with 144 test samples over 6 classes there are **24 samples
per class**, so per-class argmax accuracy can only move in steps of 100/24 = 4.1667pp. Every
downstream statistic in Weeks 2–4 was built on differences of that quantity.

A second objective emerged from the re-run: with the measurement fixed, decide honestly
which weeks are ready to write up. Weeks 0–2 are. Week 3 is not.

Parameters after the rewrite: primary measure is the mean drop in true-class softmax
probability (pp) plus the decision margin `logit[true] − max(logit[other])`, with a paired,
class-stratified bootstrap of 2000 draws at seed 20260811 and common random numbers across

plotted against its own 4.1667pp grid so the staircase is visible rather than hidden.

The guard that replaced the old `max(denom, 1e-6)` epsilon is worth stating precisely,
because the obvious version of it does not work: a selectivity ratio is computed only when at
least one *non-target* class has an effect whose bootstrap CI excludes zero **and** the mean
absolute non-target effect clears the floor. Testing the denominator's own CI instead would
be vacuous — `mean|x|` is strictly positive under pure noise, so its CI never contains zero
and such a check passes unconditionally.

Before the re-run, five extracted helper functions were exercised on synthetic data over six
scenarios (no-op, single-class knockout, all-class knockout, selective, near-floor
collateral at three epsilons, noise-only collateral) to confirm both void arms fire on
different causes and that an undefined ratio is reproducible across seeds.

### 5. Roadblocks, Failure Modes & Debugging Log

The session-1 defects, for the record, since they are what motivated the rewrite. `max(denom,
1e-6)` acted as a 4.17-million-fold multiplier whenever the non-target accuracy drop was
exactly zero, which on a 4.1667pp grid is common; the `>= 2.5` comparison then resolved on
float residue below printed precision. `R = ft_drop / base_drop` on accuracy drops could only
ever take the values 0, 1, 2 or NaN, so all six retention bands were read off a ratio of two
single samples. The caveat columns that would have exposed this stayed in notebook stdout and
never reached the exported CSVs, so the tables read as findings.

Two errors during the rewrite itself. A docstring paragraph was spliced into a function body
rather than its docstring, caught by `ast.parse` over all five cells. And the synthetic smoke
test reported `deterministic repeat equal: False` — a test bug, not a code bug: NaN != NaN,
so list equality on a list containing an undefined ratio always fails. `np.allclose(...,
equal_nan=True)` is the correct check, and an undefined ratio must be reproducible too.

### 6. Interpretability Retrospective

The accuracy numbers were never the problem, and that is the instructive part. A harness can
be exact to 1e-7 against its own ground truth and still support conclusions that are entirely
artifacts, if the statistic laid over it has less resolution than the effects it claims to
measure. The 4.1667pp grid was visible in the split sizes from the beginning.

With the measurement fixed, the science got harder rather than easier. Four things now stand
between Week 3 and a defensible transfer claim, all documented in
`docs/v3-downstream-statistics-audit.md` §8:

The most serious is that the base model's top-5 neurons damage the *fine-tuned* model more
than the fine-tuned model's own probe-selected top-5 in four of six classes — disgust by
2.5× (1.918pp versus 4.792pp). If |L1 probe weight| ranked causally important neurons, an
externally chosen set of five should not beat the probe's own pick. The careful statement is
that |probe weight| does not predict ablation damage magnitude — the two probes are fit on
different activations, so a set lying along the fine-tuned model's high-variance directions
can out-damage its own probe's pick without that probe being wrong. But the transfer analysis
assumes each model's top-5 *is* its causally important set, and that assumption is what
fails.

Second, every class returned R > 1 and every class was labelled *Substrate Preservation*.
The CONTEXT.md bands (≥ 0.80 Preservation, 0.20–0.80 Reassignment, < 0.20 Dispersion) were
written assuming R ∈ [0, 1]. Fear at R = 4.34 means the base model's neurons hurt the
fine-tuned model 4.3× *more* than they hurt the base model — the opposite of what the label
asserts. A column with one value in all six rows tests nothing.

Third, R appears to be measuring a global sensitivity difference. The fine-tuned/base ratio
of the *non-target* drop runs 1.33–1.73 across classes; five of six R values sit inside or
below that band, with happiness (1.39 versus 1.73) and sadness (1.32 versus 1.51) actually
below it. Only fear stands clearly above. The fine-tuned model is simply more fragile to
`a_cls` ablation everywhere, and R does not currently separate that from class-specific
transfer.

Fourth, top-5 overlap is at chance for half the classes. Under hypergeometric(64, 5, 5) the
expected overlap is 0.391, P(X ≥ 1) = 0.343 and P(X ≥ 3) = 0.0023. Anger and surprise (3/5)
beat chance; fear (2/5) is marginal at p = 0.045; disgust and sadness (1/5) and happiness
(0/5) do not. That coexists with cosine similarity of 0.96–0.99 on the full 64-dim
selectivity vectors — which is itself a finding worth stating: a near-unit cosine alongside
chance-level top-k overlap means the vectors are dominated by a shared component and the
top-k *ranking* is unstable. Top-k index overlap should not be reported as a substrate
measure without that caveat.

None of this is fatal to the project. Weeks 0–2 stand on their own: the audio CLS carries
class-specific causal weight, four of six classes clear a 2.5× selectivity ratio in the base
model, and the dose-response curve is measurable with real confidence intervals. Week 3 needs
one more experiment before it can be written.
