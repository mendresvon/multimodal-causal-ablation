## 6. Interpretability Retrospective

The aggregated SHAP results reveal a clear hierarchy: even after collapsing each modality's vector to a single per-sample scalar (which removes the advantage Text holds from having 1024 features vs Audio's 64), Text remains the primary driver of model predictions in this architecture (~70% total attribution). Audio provides a strong secondary signal (~25-30%), while Video remains marginal (<3%). Noticeably, fine-tuning increases Audio's relative attribution share from 23.84% to 29.77% (a +5.93% shift), indicating that adaptation to the target dataset relies more heavily on acoustic cues.

**Correction from Preliminary Analysis:** A prior entry (July 29) hypothesized Audio dominance based purely on sub-network training loss curves (Audio sub-network reached lower loss than Text). However, the paper's thesis strictly defines dominance via post-hoc feature attribution (DeepSHAP), which measures what the fused model actually relies on for its final prediction. The aggregated SHAP method applied today formally proves Text is the dominant modality, overriding the July 29 hypothesis.

**Architectural Target:** Because Text is dominant, Phase B must target the Text representation branch. Checking `src/models/e2e.py` reveals the text classification layer (`self.t_out`) accepts a 1024-dimensional input (`text_cls_dim=1024` for the 'large' ALBERT configuration), whereas Audio/Video use 64-dimensional FFN outputs (`trans_dim=64`). The Phase B probes and ablations will therefore operate over a 1024-d neuron space rather than 64-d.
